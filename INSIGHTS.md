
Actionable Business Insights
Myntra Consumer Intelligence Platform
The Big Idea
I found that I am losing ready-to-buy customers at the very final step of the checkout process, while sizing mistakes and unmet expectations drive nearly a third of shoppers to leave negative reviews. By fixing the checkout friction and updating how I display sizes, I can increase revenue using the traffic I already have, without spending more on marketing.

The Narrative Arc
The Setup: I tracked 3,920 customer journeys, read 1,400 product reviews, and reviewed 4,500 marketing records to understand how people experience the platform.

The Conflict: While my marketing brings people to the homepage and product pages successfully, the system breaks down when they try to pay. Compounding this, 31% of customers end up frustrated because the clothing they receive fails to match what they expected.

The Resolution: I must simplify the checkout screen, provide accurate size guides based on real customer feedback, and focus the marketing budget on high-performing regions and loyal shoppers.

The Core Findings
1. Where I Lose Buyers (The Checkout Bottleneck)
People love browsing items, but they stop right before paying. The homepage and product pages attract interest, but the checkout page has the worst drop-off rate on the site. Sending more traffic to the homepage will not fix revenue if people cannot easily complete their purchases.

Make buying easier by simplifying the checkout page. Get rid of any extra fields that aren't really needed. And, let people buy things without having to create an account. This way, they can just pay and go, without all the hassle.

Build trust: Display return windows and secure payment badges right next to the buy button.

Bring them back: Send a friendly reminder email or push notification within one hour to shoppers who leave items in their carts.

2. Why Customers Leave Frustrated (The Expectation Gap)
Nearly 1 in 3 product reviews expresses disappointment. The single biggest complaint (31%) is that the actual item does not match the online description or photos. This is an upstream issue with photography and sizing details, not just a product defect.

Show real details: Add exact fabric composition, physical measurements, and clear fit guides to every clothing page.

Let users rate the fit: Add a community scale showing if an item runs small, true to size, or large.

Audit bad suppliers: Flag items that receive more than 25% negative reviews and pause their listings until the supplier fixes the issue.

3. Rewarding Loyalty Over Blind Spending (Customer Groups)
I spend too much money treating every shopper exactly the same. A small group of core, loyal buyers drives the vast majority of sales, while a large group of past buyers is quietly drifting away.

Protect top shoppers: Give the most frequent buyers early access to major sales and exclusive product drops.

Nurture new interest: Send a first-purchase discount code to newer shoppers who browse frequently but haven't bought yet.

Win back drifting users: Send a targeted email with a time-limited discount to accounts that haven't logged in recently.

4. The First 30 Days Matter (Onboarding Success)
Shoppers who signed up during specific promotional months buy products significantly faster than others. The premium tier members also make their first purchases much quicker than standard accounts, proving that early communication shapes buying habits.

Look at what's working and use it: Check out the welcome emails from the months when we had the most people sign up and use those same templates for all new people who join.

Make it simple for new users to level up quickly. This way, they can become loyal members right from the start. When new users join, they should be able to reach higher membership tiers easily and fast. This helps build a strong connection with them early on.

5. Stop Wasting Ad Money on Dead Ends (Marketing Focus)
Some marketing campaigns generate thousands of views but fail to get people to click through to products. I am wasting money on content that gets looks but drives zero sales.

Shift the budget: Move 60% of the content budget into the specific ad formats that yield the highest click-through rates.

Pause underperforming ads: Instantly stop campaigns that generate high views but low clicks to redesign their creative elements.

6. Capitalizing on Hidden Regional Demand (Geography)
My traffic and sales cluster heavily in a few specific states. However, several states show high visit numbers but incredibly low purchase numbers. This highlights a clear issue with shipping costs, delivery times, or localized targeting.

Secure top markets: Guarantee fast delivery times and stock availability in the highest-revenue states.

Remove local barriers: Investigate why high-traffic states aren't buying. Check if high shipping fees or lack of local payment options are stopping them.

My Single Goal: Checkout Success Rate
This measurement gives me a clear idea of whether my solutions are effective. By increasing the number of people who finish the checkout process, I can directly boost revenue without having to spend any extra money on marketing. It's a simple way to see if my fixes are working, and it's great to know that I can make a big impact without breaking the bank.

Current State: Checkout is the largest platform bottleneck.

The Target: Reduce the number of people leaving the checkout screen by 20% next quarter.

Real-World Limitations & Next Steps
So, what we've learned so far is more like a rough idea of what's going on, rather than a precise picture of how things really are. To get a better understanding, the next step would be to use the same approach, but this time with actual data from Myntra's transactions - that way, we can get a more accurate sense of what's really happening.

I was missing some important numbers: my estimates of how much a customer is worth over time were based on the basic price of a product, not the total amount they actually paid. To fix this, I need to add a new column that shows the real revenue from each sale, so I can get a more accurate picture of what each customer is really worth.

The sentiment analyzer missed context: The basic VADER model can misclassify complex fashion reviews. Next Step: Upgrade the script to use a transformer-based language model (like BERT) to capture nuanced customer complaints.
